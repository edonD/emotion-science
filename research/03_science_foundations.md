# Emotion Science Foundations

## The 7 Universal Emotions (Ekman)
1. **Anger** — Brow lowered, lips pressed, intense stare
2. **Contempt** — Unilateral lip corner raise (one-sided smirk)
3. **Disgust** — Nose wrinkle, upper lip raise, narrowed eyes
4. **Fear** — Raised brows, widened eyes, open mouth, tense lips
5. **Happiness** — Cheek raise + lip corner pull (Duchenne smile involves orbicularis oculi)
6. **Sadness** — Inner brow raise, lip corner depression, drooped eyelids
7. **Surprise** — Raised brows, widened eyes, jaw drop

## Cross-Cultural Evidence
- Ekman & Friesen demonstrated universality with Fore tribesmen in Papua New Guinea
- Preliterate subjects with no media exposure matched emotions to faces above chance
- Replicated across Western and Eastern cultures
- Some recent challenges (Lisa Feldman Barrett's constructionist theory)
- 2023 PMC study: "Experiments on real-life emotions challenge Ekman's model"

## Micro-Expressions
- Duration: 1/25 to 1/15 of a second (40-67ms)
- Involuntary — cannot be voluntarily controlled
- Reveal concealed emotions ("emotional leakage")
- Only ~2% of expressions in high-stakes situations are micro-expressions
- Occur when person tries to suppress or conceal genuine emotion

## Training Effectiveness — The Evidence

### Positive Findings
- Training produces measurable improvement in micro-expression recognition
- More training sessions = greater improvement (dose-response relationship)
- Multimodal training (face + body + voice) shows 15% improvement vs 6% for face-only
- Recognition ability (identifying emotions) does improve with practice

### Negative Findings
- METT training: 46.3% deception detection accuracy (chance = 50%)
- Trained judges only "slightly above chance" at distinguishing liars from truth-tellers
- No evidence micro-expression training improves deception detection specifically
- Human accuracy for manual micro-expression recognition: ~47-50% even after training

### The Key Distinction
**Recognition ≠ Deception Detection.** Training CAN improve emotion recognition (identifying what someone feels). But knowing what someone feels does NOT reliably indicate whether they're lying.

## AI vs Human Performance (2025)
- AI models: 64-95% accuracy depending on dataset (CK+ = controlled, FER2013 = wild)
- Recent models: 80-84% accuracy on standardized benchmarks
- Human raters: ~73% on same image sets
- AI outperforms humans on controlled images
- Both struggle with: fear, surprise, disgust (low agreement even among humans)
- Vision Transformers (ViTs) now leading architecture for FER

## Deception Detection — The Scientific Consensus
- **No reliable non-verbal cues to deception have been identified** (meta-analyses)
- Eye gaze: 23/24 peer-reviewed studies reject eye behavior as deception indicator
- Body language myths debunked: crossing arms, touching nose, looking away — none reliable
- Human accuracy at detecting lies: ~54% (barely above chance)
- Research called "a blind alley" by PMC review (2020)
- Lisa Feldman Barrett: facial expressions match emotional state only 20-30% of the time

## Emotional Intelligence Frameworks

### Major Assessment Tools
1. **EQ-i 2.0** (Bar-On) — 5 composites: Self-Perception, Self-Expression, Interpersonal, Decision Making, Stress Management
2. **SEI** (Six Seconds) — Used in 150+ countries
3. **ECR** (Emotional Capital Report) — 10 competencies, gold standard in business
4. **MSCEIT** (Mayer-Salovey-Caruso) — Performance-based, perceive/use/understand/manage

### Core EI Components (Goleman)
- Self-awareness
- Self-regulation
- Motivation
- Empathy
- Social skills

## Key Takeaway for Our Platform
Position honestly: emotion RECOGNITION training works and has value. Do NOT claim deception detection. Focus on emotional intelligence, interpersonal effectiveness, and professional communication. The science supports recognition training — it does NOT support "lie detection" courses.
